# Houston / Harris County site standards for CRE underwriting

Reference figures that decide, on a Houston commercial site, **what can be built and what
can be re-let**. Collected because both questions come up repeatedly and both are usually
answered by guesswork.

All figures are from published municipal sources, cited below. **Verify against the
current ordinance before relying on any of them** — rates change, and the applicable
authority depends on the site.

---

## 1. There is no zoning — but there are three other constraints

The City of Houston has no zoning ordinance. That is genuinely permissive on **use**, and
it misleads people into thinking a site is unconstrained. In practice a Houston site is
usually governed by:

1. **Private deed restrictions and POA covenants** — recorded, enforceable, and often
   more restrictive than zoning would have been. These must be read; they are not
   discoverable from a map.
2. **Storm detention** (below) — the binding constraint on adding building or paving.
3. **Off-street parking** (below) — the binding constraint on changing use.

A fourth applies to specific uses: **alcohol-sales distance rules** near schools,
churches and hospitals. Texas permits municipalities to prohibit sales within 300 ft,
extendable to 1,000 ft, and Houston applies a stricter rule than the state minimum. This
routinely removes beer-and-wine convenience retail from the backfill list for sites near a
school — worth checking early, because it is often the most likely tenant category.

## 2. Storm detention

City of Houston Infrastructure Design Manual Chapter 9, effective **31 March 2021**.
**These apply in the extraterritorial jurisdiction as well as inside city limits** — so
the standard does not depend on resolving city-vs-ETJ, though the *filing authority* does.

A project site of **20 acres or less** uses the detention rate curve; larger sites
discharging directly to an HCFCD channel require HCFCD review.

Required detention rises with proposed impervious cover:

| Proposed impervious | Minimum detention rate (ac-ft/ac) |
|---:|---:|
| 0–51% | 0.75 |
| 55% | 0.78 |
| 60% | 0.81 |
| 65% | 0.83 |
| 70% | 0.86 |
| 75% | 0.88 |
| 80% | 0.91 |
| 85% | 0.93 |
| 90% | 0.95 |
| 95% | 0.97 |
| 100% | 0.98 |

For comparison, Harris County's rate for outfall to its underground storm system is
**0.65 ac-ft/ac** — lower than the City curve at every impervious level.

**1 acre-foot = 43,560 cubic feet.**

### Why this matters more than it looks

- **Detention is priced per acre of *site*, not per acre of new work.** On a 1.5-acre
  parcel, going from ~58% to ~89% impervious moves the requirement from ~1.25 to ~1.48
  acre-feet — about **65,000 cubic feet** of storage.
- **A site built before 2021 is grandfathered at materially lower rates.** Touching it may
  forfeit that. The FAQ states new scope "must use the new established detention rates"
  but does not settle whether the **whole site** must be retrofitted or only the new area.
  **Establish this with the City before assuming any paving can be added** — the answer is
  the difference between a cheap addition and an uneconomic one, and it is a free question.
- **Maintenance does not trigger detention.** Resurfacing that does not alter drainage
  infrastructure, impervious area or paving grade is exempt.
- **Dry detention basins count as impervious** for the purpose of the definition. The City
  has declined to revisit this.

### Cost of relocating detention underground

Chamber or oversized-pipe systems run roughly **$5–12 per cubic foot** of storage
installed. So freeing a surface basin on a small site is typically a **$300k–800k**
proposition before any building.

Test this against the land released before doing anything else. Land at $5–10/sq ft
against 65,000 cu ft of replacement storage means **the detention cost is commonly several
times the value of the ground it frees.** On small infill sites, developing a detention
basin usually does not pay — the arithmetic, not the aesthetics, is what kills it.

## 3. Off-street parking

City of Houston Code of Ordinances, **Sec. 26-492**. Spaces per 1,000 sq ft of gross floor
area unless noted.

| Use | Spaces / 1,000 sq ft GFA |
|---|---:|
| Furniture store | 2.0 |
| Office | 2.5 (2.75 in some configurations) |
| Clinic — medical complex | 2.7 |
| **Clinic — medical or dental** | **3.5** |
| **Retail store** | **4.0** |
| Building materials / home improvement | 4.0 (of retail sales area) |
| Shopping centre, strip, ≤25,000 sq ft | 4.0 plus increment |
| Supermarket | 5.0 |
| Veterinary clinic | 5.0 (of usable floor area) |
| Barber or beauty shop | 8.0 |
| Small restaurant | 8.0 (incl. outdoor decks, patio, seating) |
| **Restaurant** | **10.0** (incl. outdoor decks, patio, seating) |
| Hospital | 2.2 per bed |
| Nursing home | 1 per 3 beds + 1 per 4 employees |
| Church | 1 per 5 fixed seats, or 1 per 40 sq ft of main sanctuary |
| Nursery school / day care | 1 per employee on the largest shift + 1 per 5 children at capacity |

### Reading the table for re-tenanting

**Parking, not the building, is usually what limits a change of use.** A single-tenant
retail box built to the 4.0 retail minimum has **no headroom**. From that starting point:

- **Downward** — office, medical or dental clinic, furniture: all fit, often comfortably.
  Medical and dental at 3.5 is the surprise here; it is commonly assumed not to fit.
- **Upward** — supermarket and veterinary (5.0) need ~25% more spaces; salon and small
  restaurant (8.0) need double; full restaurant (10.0) two and a half times.

A useful planning factor: surface parking including drive aisles consumes roughly
**330 sq ft per space**. So each 1,000 sq ft of added building at the retail ratio needs
about **1,300 sq ft of additional lot** — which is why "we'll just extend the building"
rarely survives contact with the site plan, and why on a constrained site the parking
demand of the new use should be checked *before* the building area.

## 4. Practical sequence

1. Read the recorded deed restrictions and POA covenants. They are not on any map.
2. Establish the site's current impervious percentage and its **as-built** detention
   provision — including whether the basin was actually built as designed.
3. Ask the City whether added impervious cover triggers whole-site retrofit or new-area
   only. Free, and it determines what is reachable.
4. Only then test candidate uses against the parking table.
5. For sites near a school, check the alcohol-distance position before marketing to
   convenience retail.

!!! warning "Resolve compliance before asking for anything"
    A recorded certificate of non-compliance is a cloud on title that impairs sale and
    refinancing — and it also destroys the negotiating position for any drainage variance.
    Cure first, then ask.

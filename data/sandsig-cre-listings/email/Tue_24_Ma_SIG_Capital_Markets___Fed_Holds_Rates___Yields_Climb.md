**FED MEETING UPDATE: RATES AND OUTLOOK**

The Federal Reserve held interest rates steady at 3.50%–3.75% this week, signaling a “hawkish pause” as rising oil prices tied to escalating tensions in Iran continue to complicate the path toward its 2% inflation target.

In a move reminiscent of the 2025 tariff cycle—when markets initially feared a sustained inflation spike before conditions stabilized—Chair Powell noted that while supply shocks can be transitory, the current combination of energy costs and elevated wholesale PPI warrants a more cautious approach.

This stance was reinforced in the updated dot plot, where officials revised their year-end Core PCE forecast upward to 2.7% and maintained a “higher-for-longer” outlook, despite still projecting a median of one rate cut in 2026.

Markets responded to the lack of near-term relief, with the 5-year U.S. Treasury yield rising to approximately 4.00%—an increase of 49 basis points from its recent low of 3.51% just weeks ago in late February.

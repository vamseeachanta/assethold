**A Changing Population, A Changing Real Estate Market **

Americans Are Leaving the U.S. in Record Numbers https://email.sandsig.com/lt.php?x=4lZy~GDEIaKZDHWrz_DLheVv26Ali_DxjhhkX5TEIFCf5s_9yky.1OFz2XIhmNX2le42bHPKMnag6HV.yUy7xeFu3XAli0A0_uc

The United States is seeing a notable increase in the number of people leaving the country. Why? Over the last five years, economic, geopolitical, and social changes have made it easier or, in many cases, more appealing for Americans to live abroad. One major driver is the rising cost of living: overall consumer prices in the United States are about 25% higher than they were five years ago,
according to inflation data based on the Consumer Price Index. Baby Boomers are moving into retirement and are attracted to lower-cost countries for their golden years. Remote work has given people the flexibility to keep their U.S. jobs while living abroad. While wages may be higher in the U.S., many people feel their overall quality of life is better in other countries. As a result, more Americans are choosing to relocate abroad in search of a more affordable and values-aligned lifestyle.

This kind of population shift directly affects commercial real estate, where tenant stability is paramount. Businesses rely on population growth - or stability at baseline - as a lever for demand. When people leave, demand follows, impacting the entire economy but especially sectors like retail. Fewer people to buy from a business is an unfavorable dynamic. This shift comes at a time when office buildings are facing record-high vacancy rates leftover from the impacts of remote work and
a downsized workforce. Any office spaces are being converted into residential, healthcare, or mixed-use properties. A shrinking domestic population creates challenges for commercial real estate owners and investors, and will push the industry toward assets that are less exposed to economic risk. (The Wall Street Journal, Drew Hinshaw) https://email.sandsig.com/lt.php?x=4lZy~GDEIaKZDHWrz_DLheVv26Ali_DxjhhkX5TEIFCf5s_9yky.1OFz2XIhmNX2le42bHPKMnag6HV.yUy7xeFu3XAli0A0_uc

**Fitness Anchoring the Market **

Equinox Chairman Says 'Health is the New Luxury' as Wellness Spending Soars https://email.sandsig.com/lt.php?x=4lZy~GDEIaKZDHWrz_DLheVv26Ali_DxjhhkX5TEIFCf5s_9yky.1OFz2XIhmNX2le42bHPKMnag6HV.yky7xeFu3XAli0A0_uc

Health and fitness are booming, and gyms are growing right alongside them. As GLP-1
medications (and the soon-expiring patents on these medicines) make weight loss more accessible, the conversation is shifting from thinness to strength, performance, and longevity. Equinox is one brand leading the way, expanding its business model from gym to lifestyle destination. Memberships now extend beyond traditional gym access, offering services like personal training, nutrition and sleep coaching, and health concierge support. The brand plans to expand its footprint, opening more than 40 new clubs and a dozen hotels.

This shift is becoming increasingly visible in commercial real estate. Gym-anchored and shadowed shopping centers are seeing sustained, elevated foot traffic. Gyms and workouts are driving retail tenancy from large to small formats as both high-value, low-price gyms like Planet Fitness and Crunch, and boutique options like Orangetheory and F45 continue to expand rapidly. The impact is even felt across multi-family, where health and fitness amenities
are shifting from a nice-to-have feature to a core driver of real estate value, often allowing properties to command higher rents while maintaining lower vacancy rates. (CNBC, Robert Frank) https://email.sandsig.com/lt.php?x=4lZy~GDEIaKZDHWrz_DLheVv26Ali_DxjhhkX5TEIFCf5s_9yky.1OFz2XIhmNX2le42bHPKMnag6HV.yky7xeFu3XAli0A0_uc

**CAP Markets Monthly Update**

Treasury yields have been choppy in recent weeks, briefly falling near 3.5% before moving back higher following geopolitical tensions tied to the Iran conflict. Despite that movement, the 5 year Treasury remains in the 3.6% to 3.8% range, which lenders generally view as a stable environment for quoting debt. Energy price swings have renewed some inflation concerns, but the broader outlook remains relatively steady. Market expectations continue to point to limited policy movement,
with the FedWatch tool suggesting the possibility of one rate cut later this year. Regulators are also reviewing bank liquidity rules, which, if relaxed, could expand lending capacity and increase capital availability. Recent labor data showed softer job growth with unemployment rising to 4.4%, and investors will be closely watching upcoming inflation data for a clearer direction on where rates may move in the coming months.

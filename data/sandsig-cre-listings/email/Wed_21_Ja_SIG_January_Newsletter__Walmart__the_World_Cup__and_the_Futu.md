**What Walmart's 3D Printing Push Means For Commercial Real Estate **

Walmart and Alquist Strike Landmark Deal, Jump-Starting 3D-Printed Commercial Real Estate https://email.sandsig.com/lt.php?x=4lZy~GDEIaKZDHWrz_DLheVv26Ali_DxjhhkX5TEIFCf5s_9yky.1OFz1HQlmNT3kuk1bHPKMnaf75z7zUy7xeFu23-hiEA0_uc

3D printing has long been discussed as a future solution for commercial construction, but Walmart's recent projects suggest that the future is arriving faster than expected. The retailer partnered with Alquist 3D to build an 8,000 square foot store addition in Athens, Tennessee, now recognized as the largest 3D-printed commercial structure in
the U.S. While the first project came with growing pains, the lessons learned paid off quickly. A second Walmart addition built in Huntsville, Alabama, was completed in just seven days. Encouraged by those results, Walmart has announced plans to 3D print more than a dozen additional buildings, supported by global materials leader Sika, which will supply sustainable construction materials for future projects.

If early results hold, the implications for commercial real estate are meaningful. 3D printing has the potential to reduce construction timelines by 50 to 70 percent, accelerate return on investment, and enable more resilient and sustainable building designs. The technology is also expanding beyond retail, with residential leader ICON exploring 3D printing projects in residential real estate. While this shift requires a more specialized, tech-forward workforce, it reflects the same pressures shaping today's market: the need to build faster, more affordably, and with
greater flexibility. For investors and developers, construction innovation is shifting from concept to execution and may soon impact how quickly capital can be deployed. **Why International Travel Will Reshape Host Cities During the FIFA World Cup **⚽️

International Travel to Turn FIFA World Cup Into '10 Super Bowls' https://email.sandsig.com/lt.php?x=4lZy~GDEIaKZDHWrz_DLheVv26Ali_DxjhhkX5TEIFCf5s_9yky.1OFz1HQlmNT3kuk1bHPKMnaf75z7zky7xeFu23-hiEA0_uc

The upcoming FIFA World Cup is set to deliver a meaningful surge in travel and spending across U.S. host cities. With a 50 percent increase in the number of teams competing, international attendance is
expected to rise sharply, with overseas visitors projected to occupy roughly 40 percent of total seats, or about 1.24 million people. Hotel demand is already responding, with forecasts calling for nearly $900 million in incremental hotel revenue across host markets, an impact comparable to hosting 10 Super Bowls within a six-week window. In June alone, when the majority of matches take place, hotel room revenue is expected to increase between 7 percent and 25 percent in host cities.

For commercial real estate, the World Cup represents more than a short-term hospitality spike. Increased foot traffic will drive demand across hotels, retail, short-term rentals, and event-driven uses, while accelerating infrastructure investment and local business activity. Landlords are already exploring ways to reposition vacant space for temporary retail, pop-up venues, and vendor activation, while parking lots and underutilized land present opportunities for monetization. In select
markets, the influx of visitors may also catalyze longer-term mixed-use development near stadiums and transit corridors. For owners and investors, the World Cup offers a rare convergence of near-term income potential and longer-term redevelopment opportunity. (CoStar, Terrence Baker) https://email.sandsig.com/lt.php?x=4lZy~GDEIaKZDHWrz_DLheVv26Ali_DxjhhkX5TEIFCf5s_9yky.1OFz1HQlmNT3kuk1bHPKMnaf75z7zky7xeFu23-hiEA0_uc

**Goodbye Cubicles, Hello Flexibility** &#128188;

With Co-Working Spaces Making a Comeback, A New Era of Work Awaits https://www.thehrdigest.com/with-co-working-spaces-making-a-comeback-a-new-era-of-work-awaits/

Post-pandemic, office spaces are making a comeback, but the way companies use them has changed. Traditional layouts are being replaced by flexible workspaces that emphasize shared, open-plan areas for collaboration, networking, and informal meetings, alongside individual or shared desks. These spaces reflect the reality of hybrid work and recognize that it is no longer realistic for employees to sit in a cubicle five days a week. By reducing the amount of dedicated office space and increasing communal areas, flexible offices create environments that feel more adaptable, efficient, and aligned with how people actually work today. This shift is especially attractive to businesses. Flexible workspaces offer shorter lease commitments and greater agility in a changing in-person work environment. With employees less inclined to commit to a single company for a decade or more, businesses benefit from the ability to scale up or down as needed.

This shift is reshaping the office property sector within commercial real estate. Demand is declining for large, long-term leased office spaces and increasing for smaller, short-term leased coworking and flexible office environments. Businesses are prioritizing layouts with fewer private offices, smaller conference rooms, and larger communal spaces, particularly in prime urban locations. The global flexible office market is projected to grow by approximately 15% over the next five years, with nearly 50% of large companies expected to incorporate flexible office space into their real estate portfolios. In major metropolitan areas, flexible offices are expected to account for roughly 30% of new office leases. For owners and investors, these properties often offer higher rental yields, along with greater flexibility to scale portfolios up or down as tenant needs and employment dynamics continue to evolve. Taken together, these trends point to flexible office space becoming a more permanent fixture in commercial real estate rather than a temporary response. (Anuradha Mukherjee, HR Digest) https://www.thehrdigest.com/with-co-working-spaces-making-a-comeback-a-new-era-of-work-awaits/

**Pause in Defense Stocks Draws Investor Focus to Fundamentals **&#127981;

JPMorgan Says Buy Dip in Defense Stocks After Trump Comments https://finance.yahoo.com/news/jpmorgan-says-buy-dip-defense-112056352.html?guccounter=1&guce_referrer=aHR0cHM6Ly93d3cuZ29vZ2xlLmNvbS8&guce_referrer_sig=AQAAAICcMJWz6xa8pVdNmfBP3HN00Gif0_LFClxaFuCG0F3S5FdEq3osn1gMqeRm3Uux1_65UuTtC4lXBy-kbL6s4TDl_JnYevYrhro2lKLLr3DXSg7YDSv0Wyesv2PlJyf_oETbE5VCBH6yYZXpwQ1Bl-v-SYAJjys--dcNOXPciLeK

European defense stocks pulled back in late January, extending a third straight session of declines with shares falling as much as 4.2%. The move followed comments at the World Economic Forum that appeared to ease near-term escalation concerns around Greenland, prompting profit-taking in a sector that has delivered strong gains. Despite the volatility, we continue to view that decline as tactical rather than structural. Global defense spending remains in the early stages of a multi-year expansion, driven by persistent geopolitical tension and the need for middle powers to coordinate against coercion from aggressive superpowers. With the war in Ukraine ongoing and little indication of a near-term resolution, major institutions, including JPMorgan, continue to recommend using periods of weakness to add exposure to European defense equities.

For commercial real estate investors, these dynamics support durable, long-term demand across several property types. Rising defense budgets are driving expansion in manufacturing capacity, increasing demand for industrial facilities in established U.S. defense hubs, while continued investment in advanced military technology and cybersecurity supports demand for specialized R&D and office space in suburban office parks and high-tech corridors. Importantly, this demand is underpinned by long-term government procurement and capital commitments, rather than short-term market cycles. For investors cautious on near-term equity volatility, defense-linked real estate also offers an additional advantage: acquisitions and upgrades of U.S. industrial assets may qualify for 100% bonus depreciation, allowing investors to offset capital gains from other portfolio sales while gaining exposure to assets supported by sustained federal spending. **CAP Markets Monthly Update**

The lending environment remains steady, with minimal change in underwriting standards or lender appetite. Treasury yields have traded within a relatively narrow band, with the 5-year Treasury holding in the ~3.7%–3.9% range, reinforcing the broader narrative of rate stabilization.

At its first meeting of 2026, the Federal Reserve voted to hold the federal funds rate at 3.50%–3.75% following three consecutive cuts. Chairman Powell reiterated a cautious, data-dependent approach, noting that both inflationary pressures and labor market imbalances continue to moderate. The Fed appears comfortable pausing to assess incoming data rather than signaling urgency in either direction.

Despite ongoing political pressure and emerging leadership uncertainty at the Fed, capital markets have remained notably composed. Liquidity is available, spreads are rational, and well-structured, well-sponsored deals are clearing the market.

On the horizon, attention will increasingly shift toward the nomination of a potential new Fed chair, which could influence longer-term policy expectations. While markets are not yet pricing in a dramatic shift, the philosophical leanings of a new nominee could shape forward guidance and rate trajectory expectations into late 2026 and beyond.

For now, the message is clear: stability has returned to the rate environment, capital is deployable, and execution remains achievable for disciplined sponsors.
